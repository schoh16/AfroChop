# Food Safety Standards — DRAFT FOR COUNSEL AND FOOD-SAFETY REVIEW

**DRAFT — NOT LEGAL ADVICE OR READY FOR PUBLICATION.** Qualified food-safety professionals and counsel must adapt this standard to each jurisdiction, service type, and food category before launch.

## 1. Jurisdiction-specific approval

AfroChop must maintain an administrator-managed rule for each jurisdiction and service type, identifying whether provider approval and specific permits are required, effective dates, and an authoritative source reference. Do not assume home kitchens are authorized to sell prepared meals. When a required permit is not verified and current, block that chef/service combination from customer booking.

## 2. Provider onboarding and records

Collect only required documentation; store documents in access-controlled private storage, not public profile URLs. Track jurisdiction, service type, permit type, review state, reviewer, issue and expiry dates, and audit history. Re-check expirations and changed rules. A pending, rejected, expired, or suspended document does not satisfy a requirement.

## 3. Ingredient sourcing and traceability

Providers must identify known ingredients and major allergens for every dish, retain supplier and purchase records as required by applicable law, check packaging labels, and re-check substitutions and changed suppliers. Grocery receipts must be made available to the customer through a protected booking view and preserved according to the approved schedule.

## 4. Preparation, sanitation, and cross-contact

Providers must use safe hand hygiene, sanitation, separation, cooking, cooling, holding, packaging, and transportation practices applicable to the service and jurisdiction. They must document practical controls for shared equipment and surfaces, communicate known cross-contact risks, and not promise allergen absence without substantiation.

## 5. Booking gate

Required licensing and permit checks must pass before an eligible service is offered or booked. Allergy disclosures require a recorded provider accept/decline response before the workflow can proceed. Booking status, provider response, timestamp, and notice version must be auditable.

## 6. Monitoring, complaints, and suspension

Route food safety complaints to a trained reviewer. Mark urgent cases escalated at intake, notify the on-call safety contact, preserve records, and assess immediate listing suspension, customer communication, regulator reporting, and recall steps. Do not automatically reinstate a provider after an incident; require documented authorized review.

## 7. Local launch checklist

- Map each offered service to jurisdiction-specific requirements and authoritative sources.
- Obtain counsel/food-safety review of each service model, home-kitchen limits, permits, and records.
- Configure permit and provider verification rules before enabling bookings.
- Establish a staffed incident contact and escalation coverage before taking paid orders.
- Train staff and providers; test the allergy, permit-expiry, suspension, and incident workflows.
