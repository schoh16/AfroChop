# Food Allergy and Dietary Safety Policy — DRAFT FOR COUNSEL AND FOOD-SAFETY REVIEW

**DRAFT — NOT LEGAL ADVICE, MEDICAL ADVICE, OR READY FOR PUBLICATION.** Counsel and qualified food-safety professionals must approve this policy and the in-product controls before launch.

## Purpose and scope

This policy defines minimum marketplace controls for ingredient and allergen disclosures, customer requests, provider responses, and incident escalation. It does not diagnose allergies or guarantee that food is safe for a particular person.

## Customer disclosure and acknowledgment

Before a customer submits a service request, AfroChop must ask whether anyone consuming the food has allergies or dietary restrictions. The customer may identify known allergens and restrictions and must acknowledge the current notice version. The system records the acknowledgment timestamp and version with the request. The notice must explain that ingredients, substitutions, shared equipment, supplier changes, and cross-contact can create risks.

Do not imply that the customer must disclose a diagnosis or provide more personal information than needed to assess the request. Restrict the disclosed details to the customer, the assigned provider, and personnel with an operational or safety need.

## Provider disclosures

Each dish must have structured records for known ingredients, major allergens, and known cross-contact risks. The provider must update them when the recipe, ingredients, suppliers, equipment, kitchen, or handling process changes. Unknown or uncertain information must not be silently treated as “none.”

## Required response gate

When a booking request contains a disclosed allergy, its review status must be `pending`. The assigned provider must explicitly record either:

- **Accept:** the provider has reviewed the disclosure and represents that they can accommodate the request using their stated process; or
- **Decline:** the provider cannot safely accommodate it, with an optional explanation.

Until acceptance, no quote acceptance, booking confirmation, or payment capture may proceed. A decline cancels/stops the request. Acceptance is not a guarantee of an allergen-free outcome and must not remove the notice or disclosures.

## Operational controls

Providers must use documented ingredient checks, label checks, safe storage, clean equipment and surfaces, appropriate handwashing, separation controls, and a process for handling substitutions and customer questions. If a provider cannot confidently understand an ingredient or control a cross-contact risk, they must decline rather than guess. AfroChop must provide an accessible decline path without penalizing a safety-based refusal.

## Dish and profile warnings

Show the known ingredients, disclosed major allergens, known cross-contact risks, and a consistent safety notice on dish details, public chef profiles, booking review, and checkout. Do not use “allergen-free,” “safe for,” or equivalent language unless the specific claim has a substantiated basis and counsel/food-safety approval.

## Incidents and urgent concerns

An urgent suspected exposure or illness report must be timestamped, immediately placed into the escalated queue, and routed to the designated on-call safety contact. The reporter should be advised to contact emergency services or a medical professional where appropriate; AfroChop staff must not provide diagnosis or treatment instructions. Preserve the relevant booking, disclosures, acknowledgment version, provider response, messages, menu and ingredient data, and receipt records under the approved retention and legal-hold schedule.

## Governance

The safety lead must review serious incidents, determine whether provider suspension, listing restriction, customer notification, regulator notification, recall handling, or further investigation is appropriate, and document decisions. The schedule and responsible contacts must be completed before paid launch.
